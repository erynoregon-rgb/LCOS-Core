**Status:** Open

## What is already established

From `SU3_OPCODE_ISOMORPHISM_A1.md`:

- Natural 3-bit binary ordering uniquely preserves the fixed Gell-Mann su(3)
  commutation table under exhaustive pure-permutation search (28/28, unique,
  next best 20/28)
- Robustness confirmed across four sign/order convention variants
- Negative controls (Gray code, Hamming-weight, bit-reversed, random) all
  score at or below 20/28
- Hamming-weight ascending (11/28) is a **closed** single-transposition
  artifact — not a structural signal

The established claim is **labeling rigidity**: once the bracket, basis table,
normalization, and convention are fixed, the natural binary ordering is uniquely
compatible with the commutation table. This does not yet show that the bit
structure generates the bracket.

## What this audit investigates

Three open questions, in order of concreteness:

---

### Question 1 — Diagonal generator predicate

**Observation:** Under natural binary ordering, the two diagonal generators
(λ₃, λ₈) sit at positions 2 (010) and 7 (111). In 3-bit space, these are the
only positions with Hamming weight 1 and Hamming weight 3 respectively.

**Test:** Is the predicate "diagonal generators occupy Hamming-weight-1 and
Hamming-weight-3 positions" derivable from bit structure, or is it a post-hoc
observation?

**Method:**
1. For each of the four convention variants already tested, check whether the
   two diagonal Cartan generators (λ₃, λ₈) land at Hamming-weight 1 and 3
   positions under natural binary ordering.
2. Check whether any other compact ordering (Gray, Hamming-ascending,
   bit-reversed) also places diagonal generators at weight-1 and weight-3.
3. If the predicate holds across conventions and fails for other orderings,
   it is a bit-structural fact about the mapping.

**What it would mean if true:** The off-diagonal/diagonal partition of su(3)
generators is recoverable from a bit predicate (Hamming weight) without
generator lookup. First bit-derived structural fact.

**What it would mean if false:** The weight-1/weight-3 pattern is a coincidence
of the standard convention. The partition is not bit-derivable.

---

### Question 2 — Output index prediction

**Observation:** In the commutation relation [λa, λb] = 2i f_{abc} λc, the
output generator index c is determined by the structure constants. The question
is whether c can be predicted from bitwise operations on a and b.

**Known failure:** Pure XOR does not work. For the su(2) subalgebra:
[λ0, λ1] = 2i λ2 — indices 0 XOR 1 = 1 ≠ 2.

**Tests to run:**
- XOR: `c = a XOR b`
- XOR + 1: `c = (a XOR b) + 1 mod 8`
- OR: `c = a OR b`
- AND: `c = a AND b`
- XOR with carry (addition mod 8): `c = (a + b) mod 8`
- Bitwise majority: `c = majority(a, b)`
- For pairs where f_{abc} is non-zero for multiple c values: test whether the
  dominant c (highest |f_{abc}|) is bit-predictable even when the full output
  is not

**Success criterion:** A bit operation that predicts c for ≥ 20 of 28 pairs
without lookup. Anything below is not predictive.

**What it would mean if true:** The output index is bit-derivable. The bracket
structure is partially or wholly generated by the bit arithmetic, not just
labeled by it.

**What it would mean if false:** The output index requires lookup. The mapping
is labeling rigidity only.

---

### Question 3 — Sign prediction

**Observation:** The structure constants f_{abc} are antisymmetric. Their signs
follow from the antisymmetry plus the base values in the hand table. The
question is whether the sign of f_{abc} is recoverable from bit/chirality
relations among a, b, c.

**Connection to Gap A:** The pregeo chirality bridge (Gap A, SKOS-Core) maps
pregeo chirality (ab, xy) to Token64 bits [8:9] with a semantic alignment:
missal chirality (ab=xy) → convergent ops (SEAT, SEAL, BIND_COMMIT) and
amissal chirality (ab≠xy) → divergent ops (GHOST_RECOVER, REBRAID, SKEPTIC_GATE).

Under the su(3) mapping:
- Convergent ops (SEAT=000, SEAL=001, BIND_COMMIT=111) → λ1, λ2, λ8
- Divergent ops (GHOST_RECOVER=011, REBRAID=101, SKEPTIC_GATE=110) → λ4, λ6, λ7
- Neutral (AMBIENT=010, ECHO_BLEED=100) → λ3, λ5

**Test:** Does the missal/amissal distinction predict the sign of f_{abc} for
pairs within each class? Specifically: do missal-missal pairs have a different
sign pattern from amissal-amissal pairs?

**What it would mean if true:** Sign prediction from chirality/bit structure.
The chirality alignment from Gap A is not arbitrary — it traces the real/imaginary
split of the off-diagonal generators, which carries sign information.

**What it would mean if false:** Signs require full lookup. The chirality
alignment is a semantic convenience, not a sign-predicting structure.
